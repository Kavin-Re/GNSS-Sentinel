# GNSS-Sentinel evaluation (SYNTHETIC DATA ONLY)

Generated by `python -m gnss_sentinel evaluate`. All data come from the built-in synthetic generator; the detectors, thresholds and classifier were designed and tuned against that same generator family, so every number below is **optimistic** and says nothing about real receivers. 95% intervals: Wilson (proportions), exact Poisson (rates), percentile bootstrap over runs (AUC, medians).

Config: 200 calibration clean runs, 150 test clean runs, 100 test runs per attack, 50 training runs per class, 40 shifted-profile runs per class; 720 epochs/run at 1 Hz, onset 180-300 s, evaluation window = 420 s after onset; thresholds = 99th percentile of per-run maximum score on calibration data. The first 60 s of each run are baseline learning.

Thresholds: cn0_agc=3.83, uniformity=5.47, clock=6.46, raim=2.63, innovation=18.76, cusum=9.05

## Honest reading

* Synthetic, same-family data: thresholds and classifier were fitted on the same generator they are tested on (different seeds). Expect real-world performance to be materially worse, especially false-alarm rates.
* The shifted-profile section shows the sensitivity to modest environment change alone; real receivers also differ in AGC/C/N0 reporting, tracking loops, antenna patterns, multipath and clock steering.
* Scenario severities are drawn from ranges chosen by the author; weak-end cases (small clock steps, slow drag, weak CW) are not detectable by design and lower the detection rates.
* Detection rate of 1.00 on a synthetic scenario means 'the signature I modelled is separable', not 'this attack is always detected'. A spoofer that keeps per-satellite power structure, is geometry-consistent and also shifts Doppler is outside the model.
